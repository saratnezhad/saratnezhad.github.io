The hospital

As a biomedical engineer at Al-Zahra Medical Center I maintained the devices and worked every day in the hospital information system. What I actually spent my time on was watching nurses and doctors invent workarounds: sticky notes on monitors, settings memorised because the menu was too deep, critical values buried three screens down. The hardware was fine. The interfaces were the failure point. That was the moment engineering stopped being enough for me.

The lab habit

Before that, in a mobile-robotics lab, I built and tested electronics for rescue robots. The loop was always the same: set up a test, watch what fails, fix it, test again. I still work that way. Today the test is a usability study or an eye-tracking experiment, and the thing that fails is a design assumption.

Now

I'm finishing an M.Sc. in User Experience Design at TH Ingolstadt. My thesis, in Prof. Andreas Riener's HCI group, is a controlled eye-tracking study on whether simpler in-vehicle interfaces reduce the driver's visual demand. Alongside it I design the visual identity of the THI Startup Center. I'm looking for a role, in industry or a PhD, where the daily question is whether the design actually helps.
